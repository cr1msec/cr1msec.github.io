CJCA Exam Reflection

LearningCJCAHack The Box

10/10 flags, submitted with a day and a half to spare — and the biggest lesson had nothing to do with technique.

I was able to submit my exam with 10/10 flags completed, with approximately 1 day and 12 hours remaining.

Looking back at the exam now that it is over, I actually think it was a lot easier than I initially thought it was.

During the exam, there were definitely moments where I felt stuck or thought I was dealing with something much more complicated than it actually was. At the time, especially when I was stuck on the 5th flag, it felt like I had hit a major wall. Looking back, I think a lot of the difficulty came from not knowing what the intended path was rather than the individual techniques themselves being particularly difficult.

Day 1

I started at around 8:30 PM and was able to find three flags. Everything felt very smooth, and I was progressing much faster than I expected.

Since it was already getting late, I decided to stop around 12:00 AM and get some sleep.

At this point, the exam felt extremely easy, and I thought I would probably be able to finish the majority of it very quickly.

Day 2

I started again around 8:00 AM and already had an idea of how I wanted to approach the next flag. It turned out to be fairly straightforward, and after completing it, I thought I might be able to finish all the flags by the end of the day.

Then I reached the 5th flag.

This was where I spent most of the day. I was completely stuck and couldn't figure out where I was supposed to go next. At the time, this made the exam feel much harder than it had been the previous day.

Looking back, however, I think this was more of a problem with finding the right direction than the actual difficulty of the flag. Once I eventually understood what I had missed, the problem wasn't nearly as complicated as it had seemed while I was stuck.

Day 3

I started around 8:00 AM and was still working on the 5th flag.

I remained stuck until the afternoon, when I finally had a breakthrough. After that, the exam started flowing again.

I completed the 6th, 7th, 8th, and 9th flags afterward, which made me realize just how much of the difficulty had been concentrated around that one roadblock.

After finishing the 9th flag, I started working on the report. Most of it was completed relatively quickly, with WIN02 being the main remaining section.

At this point, I was already feeling much more confident about finishing everything.

Day 4

I finished the remaining blue team alerts, classified them, analyzed the evidence, and documented my findings.

I then finished the remaining parts of the report and reviewed everything before submitting.

With 10/10 flags completed and the report finished, I submitted the exam with approximately 1 day and 12 hours still remaining.

Looking Back

The biggest thing I realized after finishing the exam is that I overestimated how difficult it actually was.

While taking it, I had moments where I thought I was dealing with extremely difficult problems. In hindsight, most of the individual techniques were things I had already practiced. The difficult part was recognizing which technique to use and where to look, rather than having to learn something completely new.

The 5th flag is probably the best example of this. I spent almost an entire day stuck on it, and at the time it made the exam feel significantly harder. Once I found the correct path, however, I was able to complete four more flags relatively quickly.

I think the exam taught me that I sometimes make problems seem more complicated in my head than they actually are. When I'm stuck, I can start assuming that there must be some complicated technique or obscure vulnerability involved, when the answer may actually be much simpler.

Overall, I'm really happy with how I performed. I completed 10/10 flags, finished the blue team portion, completed the report, and submitted with a significant amount of time remaining.

Looking back, the exam was definitely challenging at times, but it was much easier than I expected it to be.
